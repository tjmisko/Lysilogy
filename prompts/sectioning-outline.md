This paper has a printed outline, listed below with an index for every heading. Build the map out of it: each unit is a contiguous run of outline entries, from `outline_first` to `outline_last` inclusive. The application derives each unit's pages and text boundaries from the headings, so you only decide which headings belong together and say why.

The ranges must come in order, must not overlap, and together must cover every main-text entry; front matter such as the abstract heading and back matter such as acknowledgments may be left out. Put all references in at most one unit and all appendices in at most one unit, never in the same unit as main text. A unit can start only at a heading, so a long printed section can be split only at one of its own sub-headings; a long section without sub-headings stays whole.

Grouping is the point. One unit per printed heading is a table of contents, not a reading map, and is almost never the right answer: an introduction that previews the results usually belongs with its motivation, a short background section often belongs with the model it sets up, and a run of robustness checks usually belongs together.
