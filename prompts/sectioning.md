Build a reading map of coherent, digestible topics, not a table of contents.

- Aim for 1–5 pages of actual content per unit, usually 1–2 pages. Prefer a small number of useful reading chunks. Count the content occupied: two short fragments on opposite sides of a page break are not two pages of reading.
- Choose topic boundaries first. Keep a parent topic's introduction, mechanisms, variants, examples, and qualifications together when they fit within this range. A printed subheading is evidence of internal structure, not a demand for another map region.
- For example, “Extremal Goodhart”, “Extremal Goodhart — Model Insufficiency”, and “Extremal Goodhart — Change in Regime” should be ONE unit titled “Extremal Goodhart”. Explain the two mechanisms and their difference inside its digest; do not give the parent introduction a separate sliver.
- Likewise, keep a short methods setup with its method, an experiment with its ablations, and an argument with its examples. Merge only adjacent material that answers the same clear question. Do not combine unrelated major topics just to hit a page target.
- Split a topic approaching five pages at a meaningful internal turn. Each resulting unit must stand on its own as a useful reading session. Rare shorter units are appropriate for a genuinely independent central idea or a short conclusion, but avoid runs of sub-page units. Attach brief setup or transitional text to the topic it introduces.
- Use a concise topic title rather than a breadcrumb of parent and subsection names. The summary should synthesize the whole unit; the digest should explain its internal progression and preserve distinctions between subtopics. Keep representative exact evidence for those distinctions. Merging must not discard qualifications or create a generic summary.
- Cover the target paper's substantive argument in order, without gaps or duplicate parent/child regions. Do not omit material to meet a section budget. Keep references as at most one optional navigation unit; do not divide a bibliography into chunks. Group appendices by useful topic without letting them dictate the main-text budget.
- Before returning JSON, review the complete map. Fold short dependent units into their parent or a related neighbor, split unwieldy units, and update every claim, glossary link, reading-path entry, and exact source boundary to match the final units.
